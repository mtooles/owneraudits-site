Is your business actually healthy, or just busy?

From the inside, those two feel identical. You are shipping the work, the phone rings, money moves. Meanwhile QuickBooks' 2026 report found 59% of US small businesses carrying overdue invoices, averaging $17.7K, and most of those owners would have told you things were fine.

Running a business means watching five gauges at once: the money, the customers, the leads, the inbox, and the marketing. Nobody can watch all five while also doing the work. That is not a character flaw, it is arithmetic. But it is also exactly the kind of watching that has become automatable, which is why it is worth knowing your number now.

Answer the 20 questions below honestly, from what you actually know today, not what you could look up. We score every answer, weigh the five dimensions, and email you your Business Health Score out of 100 with the plain-English read: which gauge is your weakest, what the 2026 published benchmarks say for your industry, and what your answers about AI and software spending suggest you should stop paying for.

Your score lands in one of four bands. Most owners score in the 50s and 60s.

1. Money

Where cash actually stands, not where it feels like it stands.

Do you know, without looking anything up, roughly how much is owed to you right now?
Have you personally reviewed every invoice older than 60 days within the last month?
Do you reconcile invoices against actual bank deposits at least monthly?Sent and paid are different lists. The gap between them is where money hides.
Were you paid within 45 days on average over the last quarter?

2. Customers

Concentration, quiet drifting, and who actually pays on time.

Is your biggest customer under 20% of your revenue?
Could you name the customers who used to buy regularly and have gone quiet in the last 90 days?
Do you know which customers habitually pay you late?
Does any part of your revenue repeat on a schedule, like a retainer or subscription?

3. Leads

Where buyers come from, and how fast anyone answers them.

Do you record where every new lead comes from?
Do you know which source produced your last five paying customers?
Does every new lead get a reply within one business day?63% of B2B companies never answered an inbound demo request at all (RevenueHero, 2024).
Do you know your rough lead-to-customer conversion rate?

4. Inbox

The place deals go to die quietly.

Does your business inbox reach zero, or close to it, at least once a week?
Are you certain no quote or proposal is sitting unanswered in there right now?
Do you follow up at least twice on every quote you send?Deals close on follow-up, and an unanswered quote is a decision made by default.
Do promises made over email get tracked anywhere outside the inbox itself?

5. Marketing

Whether the spending and the sending actually produce buyers.

If you run ads, do you know what a new customer costs you? If you do not run ads, was that a deliberate decision made with numbers in front of you?
Do you know your email open and click rates?
Have you compared any marketing number of yours against a published industry benchmark in the last year?
Could you say which marketing activity produced actual revenue last month?
